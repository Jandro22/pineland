# Finite-horizon causal cones and competing local-state explanations

Date: 2026-09-17  
Status: development theory note; synthetic Pineland mechanism only

## Result that needs explaining

The exact preregistered `rootedstock_execnet_ring1_15v5` screen did not close
the 30-day continuation distribution. Across 24 matched live-anchor pairs:

- 0/24 were classified equivalent;
- 14/24 were demonstrably non-equivalent;
- 10/24 were inconclusive;
- 11 of the 14 demonstrable failures included
  `final_executable_net_transport_pressure` among their non-equivalent
  outcomes.

The residual therefore remains strongly transport dominated.

## Analytic movement-mediated causal cone

Production defaults provide a useful upper envelope without any simulation.
The force-movement process is evaluated every 0.25 days. The configured daily
reallocation probability is 0.04, composed to the actual interval as

`q = 1 - (1 - 0.04)^0.25 = 0.010153599232...`.

With the default `adjacent` destination scope, one successfully issued
reallocation can advance a formation by at most one locality-graph edge. If a
formation were eligible at every opportunity and every decision became a move
toward the focal locality, then over 30 days it would see 120 opportunities.
The resulting Binomial(120, q) tail is therefore a conservative upper bound on
movement-mediated graph reach:

| Required one-edge reallocations | Upper bound over 30 days |
| ---: | ---: |
| >=1 | 0.706142 |
| >=2 | 0.344424 |
| >=3 | 0.123654 |
| >=4 | 0.034580 |
| >=5 | 0.007854 |
| >=6 | 0.001494 |

The actual model should propagate weakly more slowly because formations can be
ineligible, commands can fail, destinations need not point toward the focal
locality, and command latency, travel time, supply feasibility, interdiction,
and operational status all delay or prevent movement.

This yields a useful *probabilistic causal cone* interpretation. If state is
conditioned through graph radius `R`, a source outside that radius needs at
least `R+1` adjacent reallocations to reach the focal locality by movement
alone. Under the upper envelope, the smallest 30-day included radius with
omitted movement-mediated reach below 10% or 5% is radius 3; below 1% it is
radius 4. This does **not** prove radius 3 is sufficient for closure because
other spatial mechanisms can propagate influence, but it explains why a fixed
one-hop blanket is theoretically fragile at a 30-day horizon.

The complete horizon table is generated by
`analysis/derive_probabilistic_causal_cone.py` and frozen in
`probabilistic_causal_cone_20260917.json`.

## Alternative explanation: omitted local flow/regeneration state

The ring-1 matcher does not condition on every locally available coordinate.
Post-hoc diagnostics on the already-completed ring-1 screen were therefore used
only to nominate competing hypotheses for fresh data.

One tempting explanation can be rejected immediately: hidden movement queues.
For all 24 selected ring-1 anchor pairs, mismatch in pending incoming strength,
pending outgoing strength, and their mean ETAs was exactly zero. The failures
are not being driven by one side simply inheriting an already-issued order
that the other side lacks.

The strongest simple omitted-coordinate clues were instead:

- local net transport pressure: descriptive failure AUC ~0.636;
- local recruitment hazard: descriptive failure AUC ~0.600;
- their simple normalized mismatch sum: descriptive failure AUC ~0.686.

These values are modest and outcome-informed. They are **not** evidence that
the added coordinates solve closure. They justify only an independent fresh
development candidate.

There is also a mechanistic reason to retain both raw and executable movement
information. `insurgent_transport_net_pressures` represents expected net
movement demand, while `insurgent_executable_transport_net_pressures` weights
prospective orders by the current command-edge reliability. Two states can
therefore match executable flux while differing in the latent combination of
movement demand and command realizability that generated it. A derived inbound
realizability ratio showed a descriptive failure AUC around 0.70 on the same
24 pairs, but this too is post-hoc and small-sample.

## Two competing development hypotheses

### H_spatial_cone

Residual 30-day failure is primarily caused by source state outside the
one-hop neighborhood. If this is the dominant explanation, extending the
matched state outward in graph distance should progressively remove the
transport divergence, and the minimum useful radius should grow with forecast
horizon.

The previously frozen ring-2 contract tests the first step of this claim.

### H_local_flow

Residual failure is materially caused by omitted local flow/regeneration state
even after executable net flux and the one-hop source reservoir are matched.
The independent development candidate
`rootedstock_execnet_ring1_localflow17v7` adds local raw net transport pressure
and local recruitment hazard to the ring-1 state.

Its fresh development block is frozen in
`live_insurgency_closure_localflow_competitor_contract_v1.json` before any
fresh candidate outcomes are inspected.

## What would constitute a real theoretical breakthrough

The strongest result would not be “radius 2 works” or “two more coordinates
work.” It would be a rule for **how much state is required as a function of
forecast horizon**. A useful candidate theory is:

> Finite-horizon locality forecasts in Pineland admit an approximately local
> sufficient state whose graph radius expands with the stochastic propagation
> speed of force movement and regeneration.

The analytic movement tail supplies a mechanism-derived prior over that radius.
Fresh closure experiments can then test whether observed sufficiency follows
the predicted horizon/radius scaling, rather than searching arbitrary state
vectors.

If that scaling survives independent confirmation, it is substantially more
general and interpretable than any single successful closure vector.

## Compute discipline

The machine is shared by multiple active research/software tasks. Closure work
should therefore default to:

- static analysis and reuse of completed output first;
- two worker threads for fresh development probes;
- small development pools before any confirmatory scale-up;
- no automatic high-thread reruns while other Pineland jobs or full test suites
  are active;
- explicit user-visible justification before a large confirmatory screen.
