# Core literature audit

This file records the literature used to position the manuscript. It is a
source-verification record, not a substitute for the bibliography.

## Novelty boundary

The manuscript does **not** claim that dependency, weak sustainment, political
misalignment, or limited absorptive capacity are new findings. Those problems
are established in the security-assistance literature and in U.S. government
evaluation.

The manuscript's intended contribution is narrower:

1. selective external assistance can successfully relieve a binding military
   production constraint and thereby expand feasible operating scale or
   complexity;
2. that successful expansion can endogenously increase demand for a distinct
   complementary service faster than indigenous production of that service,
   causing the identity of the binding military constraint to migrate;
3. supported capability and independently reproducible capability should be
   treated as simultaneous state variables generated by different production
   boundaries, rather than only as present performance versus future
   sustainability;
4. constraint relief, whole-system yield, and retained indigenous capability are
   analytically distinct outcomes;
5. direct substitution and indigenous development can be observationally
   similar in short-run supported performance while diverging sharply under
   withdrawal; and
6. coordinated development may move several near-binding indigenous services
   outward together, a proposition tested prospectively in Stage 5.

## Adversarial novelty result

The September 21, 2026 novelty review materially narrows what the manuscript can
claim.

- **Binding-constraint migration is not a new general mechanism.** Theory of
  Constraints already treats system throughput as governed by a current
  constraint and expects another constraint to become limiting after successful
  elevation.
- **Successful expansion creating new shortages is not new.** Hirschman's
  unbalanced-growth framework makes complementary shortages and induced demands
  an endogenous consequence of expansion in another activity.
- **External resources creating recipient-side complementary requirements is
  not new.** Roodman, Arimoto and Kono, and Morss provide close foreign-aid
  antecedents through project proliferation, absorptive limits, recurrent
  costs, and administrative overload.
- **Supported performance versus autonomy or sustainability is not a new
  distinction in security assistance.** Karlin, Sandnes, Reynolds, and
  practitioner sustainment frameworks already occupy that conceptual ground.
- **Complementary military production and logistics constraints are not new.**
  Military-effectiveness scholarship, logistics history, and general
  complementarity theory establish those foundations.

The strongest defensible novelty claim is therefore the conjunction:

> Security assistance can be effective at its proximate task and nevertheless
> fail to increase, or even widen the gap from, independently reproducible
> capability because successful relief of one military production constraint
> expands the operating requirement and causes a different indigenous service
> to become binding.

This should be described as a **dynamic theory of military production under
external substitution**, not as a new theory of dependency or bottlenecks.

### Adversarial scorecard

| Proposed proposition | Prior-art strength | Defensible manuscript position |
|---|---|---|
| Assistance can move the organizational bottleneck | Very strong abstract precedent in Theory of Constraints and Hirschman's unbalanced-growth logic | Do not claim the generic mechanism as new; claim a security-assistance-specific production sequence under selective external substitution |
| Assistance can make complementary indigenous requirements outrun production | Strong precedent in recurrent-cost, project-proliferation, and premature-load-bearing literatures | Emphasize cross-service demand induced by successful military operating expansion rather than generic recipient overload |
| Supported capability can exceed independently reproducible capability | Clearly established substantive concern in security-assistance sustainability and autonomy scholarship | Treat the outcome distinction as prior art; contribute simultaneous production frontiers plus a causal explanation for the gap |
| Improving a nonbinding subsystem may not improve whole-system autonomy | Directly established in bottleneck and complementarity theory | Use as a system implication, not a novelty claim; the distinctive sequence is that a subsystem becomes nonbinding because assistance succeeds |

The scorecard is a drafting constraint. Future revisions should not promote any
of these component propositions into standalone novelty claims without new
evidence that changes the literature assessment.

## Verified core sources

### Biddle, Macdonald, and Baker 2018

**Citation:** Stephen Biddle, Julia Macdonald, and Ryan Baker. "Small Footprint,
Small Payoff: The Military Effectiveness of Security Force Assistance."
*Journal of Strategic Studies* 41(1-2): 89-142.

**DOI:** `10.1080/01402390.2017.1307745`

**Use:** Principal-agent explanation for limited SFA effectiveness under
interest misalignment, monitoring problems, and weak enforcement.

**Novelty implication:** The present paper should not present recipient-provider
interest divergence as a new mechanism.

### Biddle 2017

**Citation:** Stephen Biddle. "Building Security Forces and Stabilizing Nations:
The Problem of Agency." *Daedalus* 146(4): 126-138.

**DOI:** `10.1162/DAED_a_00464`

**Use:** Concise theoretical statement of SFA as a principal-agent problem and
the limits of low-footprint assistance.

### Metz 2023

**Citation:** Rachel Tecott Metz. "The Cult of the Persuasive: Why U.S. Security
Assistance Fails." *International Security* 47(3): 95-135.

**DOI:** `10.1162/isec_a_00453`

**Use:** Influence and organizational explanation for why U.S. advisers rely on
teaching and persuasion even when recipient incentives favor military weakness.

**Novelty implication:** The present paper is conditional on assistance changing
the targeted military function. It does not compete with an influence-centered
explanation of whether political reform occurs.

### Matisek 2018

**Citation:** Jahara Matisek. "The Crisis of American Military Assistance:
Strategic Dithering and Faberge Egg Armies." *Defense & Security Analysis*
34(3): 267-290.

**DOI:** `10.1080/14751798.2018.1500757`

**Use:** Fragility of externally supported militaries and the distinction
between apparent capability and durable institutional strength.

### Knowles and Matisek 2019

**Citation:** Emily Knowles and Jahara Matisek. "Western Security Force
Assistance in Weak States: Time for a Peacebuilding Approach." *The RUSI
Journal* 164(3): 10-21.

**DOI:** `10.1080/03071847.2019.1643258`

**Use:** Political and institutional foundations of sustainable security-force
development.

### Harkness 2022

**Citation:** Kristen A. Harkness. "Security Force Assistance to Cameroon: How
Building Enclave Units Deepens Autocracy." *International Affairs* 98(6):
2099-2117.

**DOI:** `10.1093/ia/iiac215`

**Use:** Demonstrates that tactically effective assisted units can produce
broader political consequences and that successful SFA can endogenously change
recipient political dynamics.

### Sandnes 2024

**Citation:** Marie Sandnes. "The Effect of Asymmetric Interdependence on the
Outcomes of Military Cooperation in the Sahel." *Cooperation and Conflict*
59(3): 405-424.

**DOI:** `10.1177/00108367231184717`

**Use:** Ownership, autonomy, and asymmetric interdependence in external
military cooperation.

### Rolandsen et al. 2026

**Citation:** Oystein H. Rolandsen et al. "Security Assistance and the Crisis of
the Liberal International Order." *International Studies Review* 28(2):
viag003.

**DOI:** `10.1093/isr/viag003`

**Use:** Current field-level positioning. The 2026 forum explicitly emphasizes
relational effects, unintended consequences, fragmentation, dependency, and
dissonance between assistance intentions and outcomes.

**Novelty implication:** A generic claim that security assistance produces
dependency would be insufficiently novel. The paper must specify the moving
production-constraint mechanism.

### Rolandsen, Dwyer, and Reno 2021

**Citation:** Oystein H. Rolandsen, Maggie Dwyer, and William Reno. "Security
Force Assistance to Fragile States: A Framework of Analysis." *Journal of
Intervention and Statebuilding* 15(5): 563-579.

**DOI:** `10.1080/17502977.2021.1988224`

**Use:** Security-sector fragmentation and recipient governance as mechanisms
that can redirect or frustrate SFA outcomes.

**Novelty implication:** The present production mechanism is conditional. It
should not assume that partner militaries are politically coherent production
systems in every fragile-state setting.

### U.S. Government Accountability Office 2023

**Citation:** *Building Partner Capacity: DOD and State Should Strengthen
Planning for Train and Equip Projects*, GAO-23-105842.

**Use:** Policy evidence that sustainment and absorptive-capacity planning remain
practical problems. GAO found that 42 of 46 reviewed Section 333 proposals did
not fully document sustainment, absorptive capacity, or measurable objectives.

**Current-status note:** GAO's public recommendation tracker reports that DOD
subsequently improved proposal guidance, while GAO continued monitoring whether
congressional notifications adequately documented absorptive capacity as of
2026. The manuscript should cite the underlying 2023 report for the reviewed
sample and avoid presenting the 2023 planning deficiency as an unchanged 2026
condition.

### Pritchett, Woolcock, and Andrews 2013

**Citation:** Lant Pritchett, Michael Woolcock, and Matt Andrews. "Looking Like
a State: Techniques of Persistent Failure in State Capability for
Implementation." *The Journal of Development Studies* 49(1): 1-18.

**DOI:** `10.1080/00220388.2012.709614`

**Use:** State-capacity bridge, especially premature load bearing and the gap
between formal or apparent capability and underlying implementation capacity.

**Novelty implication:** The manuscript should not claim that asking weak
organizations to perform beyond developed capacity is new. Its narrower claim
is that successful assistance can endogenously expand the operating requirement
and change which complementary service is binding.

### Andrews, Pritchett, and Woolcock 2017

**Citation:** Matt Andrews, Lant Pritchett, and Michael Woolcock. *Building
State Capability: Evidence, Analysis, Action*. Oxford University Press.

**DOI:** `10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198747482.001.0001`

**Use:** Broader capability-trap and problem-driven adaptation literature. It
provides a cross-domain vocabulary for distinguishing apparent performance from
durable indigenous capability.

### Milgrom and Roberts 1990

**Citation:** Paul Milgrom and John Roberts. "The Economics of Modern
Manufacturing: Technology, Strategy, and Organization." *American Economic
Review* 80(3): 511-528.

**Use:** General organizational-complementarity foundation. The paper does not
import the manufacturing model directly; it uses complementarity as an adjacent
theoretical basis for why the system return to one capacity can depend on the
levels of other capacities.

## Verified adjacent antecedents added after adversarial review

### Watson, Blackstone, and Gardiner 2007

**Citation:** Kevin J. Watson, John H. Blackstone, and Stanley C. Gardiner.
"The Evolution of a Management Philosophy: The Theory of Constraints."
*Journal of Operations Management* 25(2): 387-402.

**DOI:** `10.1016/j.jom.2006.04.004`

**Use:** Closest abstract antecedent for binding-constraint logic and sequential
constraint elevation.

**Novelty implication:** The manuscript must not claim constraint migration as
an original general systems mechanism. It uses TOC as a micro-foundation and
adds selective foreign substitution, induced military demand, and an autonomy
frontier.

### Hirschman 1958

**Citation:** Albert O. Hirschman. *The Strategy of Economic Development*. Yale
University Press.

**Use:** Unbalanced growth and induced complementary shortages.

**Novelty implication:** Successful expansion making a different shortage
salient is established outside security studies. The paper's narrower addition
is the military donor-recipient production boundary and divergence between
supported and indigenous frontiers.

### Roodman 2006

**Citation:** David Roodman. *Aid Project Proliferation and Absorptive
Capacity*. Center for Global Development Working Paper 75.

**Use:** Models donor aid and scarce recipient-side resources as complementary
inputs; project proliferation can overload local administrative capacity.

**Novelty implication:** External assistance increasing demand for scarce local
complements is not new. The paper must emphasize cross-service military demand
induced by successful operational expansion.

### Arimoto and Kono 2009

**Citation:** Yutaka Arimoto and Hisaki Kono. "Foreign Aid and Recurrent Cost:
Donor Competition, Aid Proliferation, and Budget Support." *Review of
Development Economics* 13(2): 276-287.

**DOI:** `10.1111/j.1467-9361.2008.00486.x`

**Use:** Direct analogue for donor-financed investment creating recipient-side
recurrent obligations needed to preserve project benefits.

**Novelty implication:** One of the strongest challenges to a broad
support-induced-demand claim. It belongs in the main literature intervention,
not a footnote.

### Morss 1984

**Citation:** Elliott R. Morss. "Institutional Destruction Resulting from Donor
and Project Proliferation in Sub-Saharan African Countries." *World
Development* 12(4): 465-470.

**DOI:** `10.1016/0305-750X(84)90024-X`

**Use:** Early project-proliferation account in which donor activity consumes
scarce recipient administrative capacity.

### Karlin 2018

**Citation:** Mara E. Karlin. *Building Militaries in Fragile States:
Challenges for the United States*. University of Pennsylvania Press.

**Use:** Security-assistance effectiveness and sustainability, including the
ability of a partner force to secure the state without indefinite U.S. support.

**Novelty implication:** The supported-versus-sustainable distinction is not
new. The manuscript's addition is simultaneous production frontiers and the
constraint-migration mechanism producing the gap.

### Reynolds 2025

**Citation:** Phil W. Reynolds. "Strategic Fit: Application of World Governance
Indicators to U.S. Security Assistance." *Defense & Security Analysis*,
published online December 16, 2025.

**DOI:** `10.1080/14751798.2025.2596533`

**Use:** Governance compatibility, durable capability, and an explicit Security
Autonomy Index.

**Novelty implication:** "Security autonomy" is occupied vocabulary. The paper
should claim a mechanism and measurement decomposition, not the outcome label.

### Millett, Murray, and Watman 1986

**Citation:** Allan R. Millett, Williamson Murray, and Kenneth H. Watman. "The
Effectiveness of Military Organizations." *International Security* 11(1):
37-71.

**DOI:** `10.2307/2538875`

**Use:** Military effectiveness is organizational and multidimensional.

**Novelty implication:** The paper cannot claim that military capability is not
a single stock as an original proposition.

### van Creveld 2004

**Citation:** Martin van Creveld. *Supplying War: Logistics from Wallenstein to
Patton*, 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press.

**Use:** Logistics as a real limit on feasible military operations.

**Novelty implication:** Logistics becoming binding is substantively plausible,
but logistics as a constraint is not itself novel.

### Kremer 1993

**Citation:** Michael Kremer. "The O-Ring Theory of Economic Development."
*Quarterly Journal of Economics* 108(3): 551-575.

**DOI:** `10.2307/2118400`

**Use:** Strong complementarity across indispensable production tasks.

**Novelty implication:** Improving one component while another essential
component remains weak need not raise aggregate output. The manuscript's
contribution is the dynamic assistance-induced movement of that limiting
component.

### Cohen and Levinthal 1990

**Citation:** Wesley M. Cohen and Daniel A. Levinthal. "Absorptive Capacity: A
New Perspective on Learning and Innovation." *Administrative Science
Quarterly* 35(1): 128-152.

**DOI:** `10.2307/2393553`

**Use:** Canonical organizational-learning meaning of absorptive capacity.

**Novelty implication:** Ability to absorb external knowledge or capability is
not the same as ability to reproduce the resulting whole-system output
independently.

### Knack 2001; Brautigam and Knack 2004; Moss, Pettersson, and van de Walle 2006

**Use:** Aid dependence can degrade governance or institution-building through
accountability, incentive, rent, and state-capacity channels.

**Novelty implication:** These are rival mechanisms for a supported-capability
versus autonomy gap. Historical tests must distinguish production-driven
constraint migration from institutional or political dependence.

## Positioning statement for the manuscript

The literature already explains many reasons security assistance can fail to
produce effective, legitimate, or autonomous security forces. The manuscript
asks a different conditional question: **what happens to autonomy when
assistance successfully changes the military production function it targets?**

The answer proposed and tested here is that successful assistance can alter the
partner's supported operating envelope and cause the binding service constraint
to migrate. The amount of progress depends on headroom to the next constraint,
while durable autonomy depends on whether indigenous production keeps pace with
demand at the services that bind the adapted system. Stage 5 separately tests
whether coordinated indigenous development can move several complementary
constraints outward together.
