# Historical validation protocol v1

Status: **FROZEN BEFORE SYSTEMATIC CASE CODING**

Date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

The Stage-4 experiment establishes causal relationships inside the synthetic
Pineland model. This protocol defines a separate historical exercise that tests
whether the moving-bottleneck mechanism has observable counterparts in real
security-assistance cases.

This is not a test of Pineland's numerical thresholds. It is a test of the
mechanism's **observable implications**.

## Disclosure about case selection

This protocol was written after the Stage-4 synthetic results were known. The
researcher also had broad prior familiarity with Afghanistan, Iraq, Mali, and
Colombia and had seen preliminary public material suggesting sustainment and
logistics problems in Afghanistan and Iraq. The historical exercise therefore
must **not** be described as an untouched preregistered confirmation.

To reduce selection bias, the protocol freezes the questions, coding rules,
case set, source hierarchy, and disconfirming-evidence requirements before
systematic case coding begins.

## Case set

The initial four-case set is:

1. **Afghanistan, Afghan National Defense and Security Forces / Afghan National
   Army**, with particular attention to force generation, contractor-enabled
   maintenance, aviation, logistics, and the consequences of withdrawal.
2. **Iraq, Iraqi Security Forces**, with particular attention to rapid force
   generation, logistics and sustainment development, command, and the period
   surrounding the 2014 collapse of major regular-army formations.
3. **Mali, Malian Armed Forces and external training missions**, as a harder
   case where political fragmentation, coups, multiple providers, and divergent
   threat perceptions may dominate the production mechanism.
4. **Colombia**, as a comparatively successful or at least substantially more
   capable long-run security-force development case that can test whether
   indigenous institutions and sustainment kept pace with externally enabled
   capability.

The four cases are selected for variation in outcomes and political context,
not to estimate a population-level treatment effect.

## Unit of analysis

The preferred unit is an identifiable **assistance episode x military service
constraint x time period**, not the country as a single undifferentiated case.

Examples include:

- training expansion followed by a sustainment shortfall;
- command reform followed by a logistics bottleneck;
- external maintenance enabling a fleet beyond indigenous maintenance capacity;
- indigenous logistics development that allows externally enabled capability to
  persist after donor drawdown.

## Observable implications

### OI1. Initial constraint

Before or early in the assistance episode, credible sources identify a military
service or institutional function as materially limiting operational output.

### OI2. Targeted relief

External assistance materially increases the supply or quality of the targeted
service, or produces a documented increase in the operational output associated
with it.

### OI3. Operating-envelope expansion

Following relief, the force increases scale, tempo, geographic reach, platform
utilization, formation availability, or another demand-generating dimension.

### OI4. Constraint migration

A different service subsequently becomes a documented limiting factor, or the
same service remains limiting despite large external substitution.

### OI5. Indigenous replacement

The evidence distinguishes externally supplied service from indigenous
production or sustainment. The strongest support for the theory occurs when the
trajectory of indigenous replacement helps explain whether capability persists
after support changes.

### OI6. Withdrawal or support shock

When external support falls, capability should degrade disproportionately where
the supported operating requirement exceeds indigenous production at the
binding constraint. Where indigenous replacement kept pace, capability should
be more resilient.

## Coding fields

Each episode receives the following fields:

| Field | Coding |
|---|---|
| Case | Afghanistan, Iraq, Mali, Colombia |
| Episode dates | Start and end, with uncertainty if necessary |
| Initial candidate bottleneck | force generation, logistics, command, maintenance/aviation, other |
| Evidence strength for initial bottleneck | 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 moderate, 3 strong |
| Assistance type | substitution, development, hybrid, ambiguous |
| Targeted function improved | yes, partial, no, unclear |
| Operating demand expanded | yes, partial, no, unclear |
| Post-relief bottleneck | coded service or unclear |
| Bottleneck migration supported | yes, no, unclear |
| Indigenous replacement evidence | positive, negative, mixed, unclear |
| Withdrawal/support shock | present, absent, unclear |
| Predicted autonomy response | increase, decrease, little change, indeterminate |
| Observed post-shock capability response | improve, stable, degrade, collapse, indeterminate |
| Major rival explanation | politics, corruption, patronage, threat divergence, external shock, other |
| Theory fit | strong, partial, weak, contradictory |
| Source quality | primary official, oversight/audit, peer reviewed, high-quality secondary, other |

## Source hierarchy

Sources should be used in this order where available:

1. official audit and oversight reports with documented evidence, including
   SIGAR, GAO, DoD Inspector General, parliamentary or equivalent reports;
2. contemporaneous official records and declassified documents;
3. peer-reviewed scholarship using primary evidence;
4. high-quality research institutions with transparent sourcing;
5. reputable journalism for facts unavailable elsewhere.

Claims should not rest on anonymous commentary when a stronger source exists.

## Disconfirming evidence requirement

For every episode coded as supporting bottleneck migration, the case memo must
record the strongest available evidence for at least one rival explanation.

The mechanism is weakened when:

- the claimed new bottleneck clearly predated assistance and did not materially
  change;
- the targeted function did not actually improve;
- capability changed primarily because the adversary, political leadership, or
  force composition changed independently of the assistance mechanism;
- external withdrawal coincided with a major shock sufficient to explain the
  outcome without reference to indigenous service production;
- the observed service deficit is only asserted retrospectively without
  contemporaneous evidence.

## Interpretation rule

Historical evidence can support three levels of claim:

1. **Plausibility:** the predicted sequence appears in one or more cases.
2. **Process support:** temporally ordered evidence supports the proposed causal
   sequence and weakens major rivals in a case.
3. **Cross-case consistency:** the mechanism predicts meaningful variation
   across supportive and deviant cases.

This exercise cannot establish a general causal effect of security assistance
without a separate identification strategy.

## Freeze rule

The fields and case set above should not be changed after systematic coding
begins unless an amendment is recorded in a dated appendix explaining why the
change was necessary and whether it was outcome-informed.
