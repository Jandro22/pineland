## Cross-case interpretation

The preliminary cases do not support a single deterministic sequence. They are
more useful because they separate different parts of the proposed mechanism.

**Afghanistan** shows that a constraint can remain latent beneath continued
external substitution. Supported aviation capacity expanded, but indigenous
maintenance and logistics did not replace the external service fast enough.
The later support shock therefore tests retention more directly than migration.

**Iraq** provides the clearest evidence for sequential constraint change. Large
force-generation achievements coexisted with support institutions that lagged
the growth of fielded formations. The production sequence is well documented,
but it is embedded in severe political and organizational problems.

**Mali** is the strongest warning against overstating migration. Training
improved local skills, but many complementary constraints were already visible.
The case supports local-system divergence more clearly than endogenous movement
of the binding constraint.

**Colombia** demonstrates that supported capability need not remain permanently
dependent. External pilots, mechanics, maintenance, logistics, and operating
costs were progressively replaced through a long nationalization process. The
case therefore supports the retention side of the framework and provides a
positive scope condition for the argument.

## What would weaken the historical contribution

The cross-case historical contribution should be narrowed if later source audit
shows any of the following:

1. the apparent initial constraint was incorrectly identified;
2. the targeted function did not materially improve;
3. the alleged later constraint clearly predated the intervention without any
   change in relative importance;
4. the support change is inseparable from a larger shock that fully explains the
   outcome;
5. indigenous replacement cannot be distinguished from continued external
   production;
6. a political or adversary-side explanation accounts for the temporal sequence
   better than the production mechanism.
